# Production Log 011 — The shoestring, priced

*2026-11-02.*

**Priced:** the production's entire fixed
budget, tallied in the open, and set against
what it has shipped and what the field pays.

**The tally (subscriptions only):**

- Muse AI: $20/month
- Grok: $30/month
- Google AI Pro: $20/month
- Suno: $10/month
- CapCut: $20/month
- Frame Grabber $20/year, Taio $15/year

**$102.92/month effective. $1,235/year.**
Variable top-ups (extra usage credits, bought
as needed) are excluded; the previous entry
([Production Log 010](prodlog_010.md)) covers
that mechanism. "Shoestring" here is literal:
this is the number, next to what other AI
movie makers run on.

**The scope:** the same tally also covers all
of the director's everyday AI use: personal
assistant, research, writing papers, essays
and books, image generation, other personal
use. The movie-making share is therefore much
less than the headline; no split is claimed.

**What it shipped** (official slate,
2026-10-21 to 2026-10-30; counting rule:
shipped public pieces only): EMBER, the first
official teaser (1:00); the Main Title opening
credits (1:03); T02 THE WAIL, the second
official teaser (1:12); THE LITTLE BLADE
HOLDS, the kaiken kata proof of concept
(1:29). Four pieces, 4 minutes 44 seconds.

Charged against the whole month anyway:
**$25.73 per piece, about $21.74 per finished
minute.** Even a full year charged to the
same four pieces stays under the field's low
end per finished minute.

**What the field pays** (researched the same
night; no census of AI filmmakers exists, so
these are published pricing and documented
production budgets, a range, not a survey
average): a DIY short-film tool stack runs
about $10–200/month, a complete working stack
$100–300/month before credit top-ups.
Documented AI shorts run **$750–$5,000
all-in, about $315–$750 per finished
minute**, with video generation credits at
70–85 percent of spend. The structural
difference: the field pays for dedicated
video generators on top of its language-model
plans; this production pays for none — video
rides inside the flat subscriptions above.

**Why the number is this low:**

1. **No API prices.** Flat subscriptions
   only; the per-generation meter that makes
   up most of the field's spend never runs.
2. **Methodology.** Brief before frames,
   storyboard and scorecard in advance, canon
   locks on every generation call, cheap
   lanes iterate and scarce lanes get locked
   prompts only, minor tweaks fixed in the
   edit instead of regenerated, an audit
   gate before anything ships.
3. **Constraint as engine.** The production
   limits itself to an iPhone (no desktop,
   no laptop) and to the weekly usage limits
   of those flat plans. Frustrating at times,
   and the point: the constraint forces the
   efficiency. In the director's words: "The
   more efficient we become, the more
   techniques we develop, and the faster and
   more stuff we ship."

**Why it exists at all:** this production is
artisan work. It is not done for money. It is
done for the craft, and for the love of
learning and expression. The shoestring is
not a hardship the work survives; it is the
shape the work takes when money was never
the point. That is also why the streaming
services went: the HBO and Hulu cancellations
in [Production Log 010](prodlog_010.md) were
this choice, made the day before the tally.
The reflection is [Director's Note
009](../directors-notes/dirnote_009.md),
"The streaming services died for the tokens":
the money moved from watching to making.
