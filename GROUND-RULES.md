# Ground Rules — Yellow Rav3n GitHub Repo

Version 1.0 (draft), drafted 2026-10-21 by Fluffy from the author's brief in the
"⛓️🐦‍⬛ Yellow Rav3n: A Blockchain for Prior Art" chat.
Status: RATIFIED by the author at repo creation, 2026-10-21 (the
creation session moved up from the planned Sun Oct 25 session at
the author's order).

The author's brief, in his words: the repo houses the public history of
Yellow Rav3n, because her character development was done in public via
Twitter/X. We do not put everything in it — not the entire story arc and
canon. The repo is our calling card and our prior art evidence. It also
houses a documented pointer to all the episodes (Twitter/X and YouTube).
Sample storyboards and a lookbook may go in. We do not show the entire
kitchen and secret ingredients.

Amendment, same day, also in his words: the repo is also the official
site (there is no website yet) — it will serve as blog, development log,
announcements, and a repository for documents and PDFs; moving forward
it is the official public log and the index to the historical links
posted on YouTube and Twitter/X.

## 1. What this repo is — the official site

There is no Yellow Rav3n website (yet). This repo IS the official site:
her home base on the public internet, under the author's own control.
It is also the official communication channel for A Centaur Production:
if an announcement does not appear here, it is not official, whatever
any platform post says. (Outbound channel: announcements flow from
here; inbound conversation still happens on X, per §10.)

The site renders through GitHub Pages, free, from this same repo —
one source, two faces (repo view, web view), no separate website to
maintain and nothing extra to pay. That fits the standing shoestring
directive: every step of the workflow runs from an iPhone. Pages
setup (publishing source, BLACKFIRE styling) lands at the creation
session; the repo's markdown is the site's content from day one.

It does four jobs:

1. **Calling card.** A stranger, a platform, a collaborator, or a producer
   landing here understands who Yellow Rav3n is, what she looks like,
   and that she is a real, ongoing live-action series — inside two minutes.
2. **Prior art evidence.** A dated, hash-chained public record of her
   creation and development: genesis 2023-01-24, the tool-by-tool
   evolution, the design locks, the launch. If a copy ever surfaces, this
   repo is where the dates do the talking.
3. **Official public log.** Moving forward, this is the official blog,
   production log, and announcement surface for Yellow Rav3n and for
   A Centaur Production — the same repo-as-blog pattern the author already
   runs for Trillions Per Second, where open commits are the
   publishing surface.
4. **Document and artwork repository, and master index.** The home for
   public documents and PDFs (production reports, press material,
   slides), the repository of finished public artworks, and the
   official index to the historical links posted on YouTube and
   Twitter/X. Platforms hold the videos; this repo holds the map.

Anything that serves none of these jobs does not belong here,
however cool it is.

## 2. The public record principle

Yellow Rav3n was developed in public. For everything up to the launch,
the repo **mirrors** that public record; it never scoops it and never
extends it.

- Historical material goes in only after it is public somewhere (X
  or YouTube — the two platforms this repo acknowledges; platform
  scope set by the author, 2026-10-21, and expandable only by his
  order) — or the author explicitly clears it to debut here.
- The repo adds no new canon. Canon is ratified elsewhere (the private
  vault) and the repo records the public shadow of it, at published depth.
- Episodes and videos still publish on their platforms first (X,
  YouTube); the repo indexes them. Text is the exception: blog posts,
  production log entries, announcements, and documents may be **repo-native** —
  published here first, like the author's TPS repo — because the repo
  is now the official log (see §11).
- Platform timestamps are the primary evidence; the git commit chain is
  the consolidated, tamper-evident copy of that evidence.

**The slow reveal (author's doctrine, 2026-10-21).** This is a
slow-burn, slow-reveal series, run like a Netflix show: versions of
her and layers of her universe that she has not shown yet exist in
the author's plans long before the audience hears of them, and each
is revealed only when its episode or official trailer ships
publicly. (This file names no examples, deliberately: naming a
planned version here would break the rule this paragraph states.
The author's examples live in the private record only.) The repo is
built to receive those reveals without restructuring: indexes and
entries carry register/version dimensions from the start, so a new
version slots into `characters/`, `episodes/`, `artifacts/`, and
`artworks/` as new entries the day it ships. But the repo never
pre-announces, pre-folds, or hints: no placeholder folders, no
"versions" taxonomy naming unshipped versions, no coming-soon lists.
Until a reveal ships, the repo behaves exactly as if it didn't
exist. Flexibility is structural; secrecy is total.

## 3. The Kitchen Test (admission gate for every file)

A file is admitted only if it passes all three questions:

1. **Already public, cleared to debut here, or authored as a repo-native
   text post** (blog / production log / announcement / document, per §11)?
   If none of these: out.
2. **Finished and ratified?** Drafts, experiments, half-locks, and
   work-in-progress notes stay in the private vault.
3. **Does it prove we made her, or does it teach someone else how to?**
   Proof goes in. Recipe stays out. If a file does both, publish a
   redacted or excerpted version that keeps the proof and drops the recipe.

When in doubt, leave it out. A later commit can always add; a public
commit can never be taken back (forks and clones keep copies).

## 4. What goes in

- **Timeline / evidence chain** — the dated milestones, newest first in
  the README, full chain in [`evidence/EVIDENCE-INDEX.md`](evidence/EVIDENCE-INDEX.md).
- **Episode index** — the documented pointer to every episode and video
  (see §7). Links out to X and YouTube; nothing is hosted here.
- **Calling-card character material, at published depth** — who she is,
  the BLACKFIRE Live-action design language summary, the Wingspan kamon
  and wordmark, the house style line (live-action Hollywood, Kill Bill
  in ancient Japan). Written for a reader, not lifted from canon files.
- **Characters** — the cast directory, `characters/`: one entry per
  character in her universe, at published depth. **The appearance
  gate (author's rule, 2026-10-21): a character gets an entry only
  once they have appeared in an official episode or an official
  trailer.** Development posts, experiments, and the private story
  arc do not qualify. No placeholder entries, no "upcoming cast"
  lists, no pre-announcing — the directory's silence about a
  character is itself spoiler discipline. **On-screen only
  (author's ruling, 2026-10-21): appearance means seen on screen;
  a voice-only appearance does not qualify** — EMBER's two heard
  voices (the bystander, the Demon) get no entries until they are
  seen. First entry: Yellow Rav3n (EMBER, the official series
  introduction, 2026-10-21).
- **Samples** — excerpted lookbook pages and storyboards of *released*
  work only (see §6).
- **Production history** — public-safe documents of record, e.g. the
  AI Movie Tools Summary Report (2026-10-21, sanitized edition).
- **Blog, production log, and announcements** — repo-native text,
  per §11: dated posts in `posts/`, numbered production-log entries
  in `production-log/`, milestone announcements.
- **Documents and PDFs** — public documents of record in `documents/`:
  production reports, the sample lookbook PDF if the author elects it,
  press material, slides and decks. Only the public-safe edition ever
  ships; the internal source document stays home (precedent: the AI
  Movie Tools report's sanitized PDF ships, its internal markdown
  does not).
- **Artworks** — the finished public artwork repository in `artworks/`:
  key art, posters, covers, title cards, and released stills at web
  resolution, kamon + byline stamped. Finished and released only;
  canon reference images are kitchen, not artwork, and never land here
  (see §5).
- **Artifacts** — the catalogue of her in-world objects in `artifacts/`:
  weapons, attire, and gear (the katana and its saya, the golden sakura
  kimono and black obi, the Tranquil Solitude red sensu fan, the
  kanzashi and hairpin). One entry per artifact: its name, what it is
  in her story, finished stills of it from released work, and its first
  public appearance (episode index link). Three neighbouring folders,
  three different things: `artworks/` holds pictures, `artifacts/`
  catalogues the objects in her world, `samples/` holds excerpts of
  larger works.
- **Credits** — per episode, in full. House rule (2026-10-20): credit
  the original authors — for a remix, cover, or edit, name the original
  performer, songwriters, and producer alongside the derivative artist.

## 5. What never goes in

This mirrors disclosure tier T3 of the YR Handoff Protocol v1
(the private YR Handoff Protocol v1). No exceptions
without an explicit author order naming the item.

- The entire story arc, future scripts, and unreleased episode material
- Season 1 twist and the shadow-integration mechanics
- Rift / Prime / Legacy rules beyond what public posts already show
- Demon Mode: the full-expression look, its canon references, and the
  transformation recipe (published posts show what they show; the repo
  adds nothing beyond them)
- The Prompt Library, prompt lock blocks, master prompts, and assembled
  per-shot prompts — including prompt text hidden in metadata or in
  generation sidecar files (no `media-generation-*.json` files, ever)
- Raw reference packs, the face/reference stack, full-res canon masters
- The canon folder, the Context Node, vendor briefs, the Handoff Protocol
- The full soundtrack ledger (per-episode public credits only, per §4)
- Storyboards, boards, or stills for unreleased work
- Unshipped versions, registers, and archetypes — including the fact
  that any specific one is planned (see §2, the slow reveal). Public
  posts show what they show (e.g. the LEGACY node, anime-era posts);
  the repo adds no version, and names none, before its ship day.
- Unreleased original score — the YR Theme and any Suno-composed
  score exist in production long before they are heard; no score
  audio, takes, or score announcements land here before the release
  that carries them (see §7).
- Anything identifying the author beyond the ~C4Chaos name

## 6. Samples: lookbook and storyboards

Samples are the menu photo, not the recipe.

- Excerpts only: a few spreads of the lookbook, never the full volume;
  a few boards from a *released* piece (e.g. the EMBER teaser), never
  boards for anything unreleased.
- Web resolution only; no layered/source files; metadata stripped.
- Every sample carries the Wingspan kamon and the Centaur byline
  (~C4Chaos / Fluffy) — the stamp is part of the evidence.
- One hard test per sample: if a competent stranger could rebuild our
  pipeline or her exact look from this sample alone, it is not a sample,
  it is a leak. Cut it down or keep it home.

### Artifacts: show, don't spec

An artifact entry *shows* the object in finished, released frames and
tells its story at published depth. It never *specifies* it: no
continuity reference sheets, no orbit/turnaround packs, no construction
or generation detail, nothing a stranger could use to replicate her
exact katana, kimono pattern, or tattoo draftsmanship. The reference
stack that keeps her consistent across episodes is the Continuity Five
working material — kitchen, per §5 — and an artifact entry pointing
at it does not change that. Same object, two depths: the museum label
is public, the workshop manual is not.

## 7. The episode index

One canonical table ([`episodes/EPISODES.md`](episodes/EPISODES.md)) pointing to everything
she has publicly appeared in. One row per piece:

`Date · Title · Series/register (Demon Mode, Tranquil Solitude, MTV,
LEGACY, teaser, …) · X link · YouTube link (where one exists) ·
Duration · Soundtrack credit (full,
per §4)`

Rules: a link is added only after it is verified against the logs —
never from memory, never guessed. Source of truth stays the private
x-posts log; this index is its published, curated cut.

**Soundtrack credits — borrowed vs. original (author's order,
2026-10-21).** Two kinds of music, two credit forms, everywhere a
track is named in this repo (episode index, X post repository,
posts, production log):

- **Borrowed tracks:** credit the full chain, always — the cover,
  remix, or edit artist *and* the original artists: original
  performer, songwriters, and producer where known and verified in
  the soundtrack ledger. Never credit only the derivative artist.
  This is the house rule ("we credit the original authors in this
  house!", 2026-10-20) applied to every borrowed track, because we
  respect the copyright of the works we borrow. The repo links
  episodes; it never hosts borrowed audio (§10).
- **Original score:** A Centaur Production also composes its own original
  music with Suno — including the YR Theme, in production now. When
  an original score ships in an official episode or trailer, it is
  credited as what it is: original score by A Centaur Production (composed
  with Suno).
  Until it ships, the score is unreleased work under the slow
  reveal (§2/§5): no audio, no takes, no announcements.

The X post repository ships here too (author's order, 2026-10-21):
[`episodes/X-POST-REPOSITORY.md`](episodes/X-POST-REPOSITORY.md), the public edition of the private
X post log — every dated post, every era, with the soundtrack index.
It is a public EDITION, never a copy of the working log: internal
annotations stay home (background checks, fetch notes, production
file references, continuity keys, draft bookkeeping). Maintenance
rule: the repository is kept up to date — every new Yellow Rav3n
post is appended to the private log and to the public edition as it
ships, and the two are reconciled at each repo session.

## 8. Evidence and link-rot rules

- Every dated claim in the repo carries its platform link. No link,
  no claim — an unverified ID stays out until confirmed (see the open
  items in [`evidence/EVIDENCE-INDEX.md`](evidence/EVIDENCE-INDEX.md)).
- Links die: posts get gated, accounts change, platforms fold. Each
  milestone row is backed by a dated screenshot or archived snapshot
  kept in `evidence/` — the link is the pointer, the snapshot is the
  proof of what it pointed to.
- Withheld milestones (work that proves the chain but cannot be shown)
  may be recorded as SHA-256 hashes with dates only. A hash proves we
  held the file on that date and discloses nothing about its contents.

## 9. History integrity

- Never rewrite published history: no force-push, no rebase of pushed
  commits, no deleting a mistaken commit. Corrections land as new
  commits that say what they correct. The chain is the product;
  a gap or a quiet edit would devalue every other link in it.
- Commit messages carry the milestone date and a plain-language summary.
- Era tags mark the chain's chapters (e.g. `era-genesis-2023`,
  `era-consistency-2025`, `era-launch-2026`).

## 10. Interaction and licensing

- Read-only by design: this is a record and a calling card, not a
  community project. No contributions, no pull requests, no fan-work
  license. GitHub Discussions stay off.
- Licensing: the three-layer split adopted by the author 2026-10-21,
  mapped in [`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md) — Core IP All Rights Reserved
  ([`LICENSE-ARR.md`](LICENSE-ARR.md)); designated Share Pack material under CC BY-NC
  4.0 ([`SHARE-PACK.md`](SHARE-PACK.md), starts small, designation by name only, CC
  grants are irrevocable); code under MIT ([`LICENSE-MIT.md`](LICENSE-MIT.md)); the
  IP & Training Notice over everything ([`IP-AND-TRAINING-NOTICE.md`](IP-AND-TRAINING-NOTICE.md):
  no model training, no likeness replication, agent use is its
  principal's use). Trademarks (YELLOW RAV3N™, Wingspan kamon,
  wordmark) are never licensed here. This repo is a deliberate
  exception to the house CC BY 4.0 default. Publication here is
  evidence, not permission.
- Forks: accepted, on the record (author's call, 2026-10-21). A public
  repo cannot disable forking, and a private repo would kill the
  site, the calling card, and the public evidence chain, so forks
  stand. The stance, written into [`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md) ("On forks and
  copies"): a fork is a platform mechanic, not a license; it grants
  no rights; it preserves our commit history, timestamps, and origin
  attribution, so it extends the evidence chain rather than breaking
  it. The real wall stays the Kitchen Test — the forkable surface is
  finished web-res work only, the recipe never enters the repo.
  Enforcement follows use (commercial exploitation, false claims of
  authorship), not copying: copyright claim, trademark claim, and
  platform takedown processes.
- Videos and music are linked, never hosted: no mp4s of episodes, no
  soundtrack audio files. This keeps the repo light and keeps other
  artists' copyrighted recordings out of our archive.

## 11. Editorial: blog, production log, announcements, documents, artworks

Repo-native publishing follows the same fences as everything else here,
plus three of its own:

- **One log, named Production Log.** Not two logs. The public making-of
  record is the Production Log (`production-log/`), the published-depth
  cut of the private Production Evolution Log. "Devlog" is software
  language and stays with the TPS repo; Yellow Rav3n is a film
  production, its channel is A Centaur Production, and one log is all a
  shoestring, one-phone operation should maintain. Blog posts and
  announcements are audience-facing and live in `posts/`; the
  Production Log is the dated making-of record. Different jobs,
  no overlap, no double-posting.

- **Depth, not diary.** The production log records production at published
  depth: what was made, what changed, tool and workflow evolution in
  the open (the public cut of the private Production Evolution Log's
  arc). It never carries prompts, lock text, unreleased plans, or
  canon-in-progress. If an entry would fail the Kitchen Test, it stays
  in the private log.
- **Announcements announce public things.** A launch, a new episode,
  a milestone, a document release. Nothing is announced here before
  its public moment on its own platform, and no announcement teases
  withheld canon (Demon Mode's full look, the S1 twist) beyond what
  the author has already chosen to show.
- **PDFs ship sanitized, and get read before they ship.** The rendered
  public PDF is the deliverable; its internal source never commits.
  Before a PDF lands, its text is checked for leaks (prompts, private
  names, T3 terms) — a PDF's text layer remembers what its pages hide.
- **The name (author's convention, 2026-10-21).** In documents and
  body text, her name is written **Yellow Rav3n** — normal caps,
  capital Y, capital R, the 3 stays a 3. Not YELLOW RAV3N, not
  Yellow Raven (spoken form only), not yellow rav3n. ALL CAPS
  belongs to display: graphics and titles render the name in the
  official YR Vengeance lettering (the house wordmark art), and
  headlines may follow it in caps. Two carve-outs: (1) quotations
  stay verbatim — a published post titled "MEET YELLOW RAV3N." is
  quoted exactly, caps and all, because the record is evidence;
  (2) trademark and legal references may style the mark in caps
  (YELLOW RAV3N™) where the license files do. Historical material
  is never rewritten to comply; the convention governs everything
  written from 2026-10-21 forward.
- Housekeeping: posts are dated files, newest linked at the top of the
  README's "Latest" section; major announcements also get a git tag,
  so the announcement itself is a dated object in the chain.
