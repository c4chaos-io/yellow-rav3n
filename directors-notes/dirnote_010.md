# Director's Note 010 — Why she gets an evolution montage

*2026-11-20.*

People keep asking how Yellow Rav3n was made, as
if there was a day we sat down and made her.
There was not. There was a concept post on
Jan 24, 2023, and then almost four years of her
surviving her tools.

That is the honest frame for this montage, and I
said it plainly back in October: the evolution
of Yellow Rav3n is the evolution of generative
AI. Watch the cut with that in mind. The crude
anime frames are not an early style choice, they
are what the medium could do in 2023. The drift,
the faces that are close but wrong, the motion
that melts: that was the medium in 2024. The
moment she starts holding, render after render
in August 2025, you are watching a capability
arrive in the industry and land on one character
in real time. She is a longitudinal record of
the medium, dated post by post, because she
never changed projects. The project kept
changing under her.

The bet underneath it has not changed since the
first post. That post had no story and no
performer. It said, in full: concept character.
Characters are what audiences carry home. Nobody
carries a world home with them, they carry a
character. So we built the character first, took
the slow part slowly, and let the world come
second. Slow burn, slow reveal is not a
marketing tempo for us. It is the production
reality of doing this properly on a shoestring,
on an iPhone, with no studio and no crew. And
the shoestring was a choice, never an apology.
We use the frontier labs, Grok, Gemini and
Muse, but only through ordinary subscriptions
on an iPhone, never their APIs. Because we would
not buy our way out, we had to develop a
methodology instead. The constraint produced the
method, and the method is now the signature.

Which is why the locks at the end of this story
matter more than any single clip in the montage.

Locking her face in the Standard Canon on Nov 3
was the day she stopped belonging to whichever
model we happened to open that morning. Before
the lock, every tool had an opinion about who
she was, and half our work was rejecting her
cousins. A near match is a failed render in this
house, however pretty it is. After the lock,
there is one face, one reference stack, one
standard every lane has to meet. The tools are
interchangeable vendors now. She is not.

Locking the world in November was the same
decision at the other end. A home ground, an
era, an opening year: 1469, the Kyoto mountain
fringe in the Onin years. Until that was
settled, every new clip risked quietly
rebuilding her world to fit the clip. Now the
world holds still, costume and place held strict
to the era, and the story gets built on ground
that does not move.

EMBER sits at the end of the montage for a
reason. It was the first trailer, the first time
the early concept, the consistency work, the
house style, and the music all showed up in the
same minute and held. Everything before it in
the cut is the road to that minute. Everything
after it, the teasers, the locks, this montage
itself, is us refusing to let that minute be an
accident.
Soundtrack: "Set Fire to the Rain (Hoodtrap
Mylancore) SLOWED" by itsDRYKO, a hoodtrap remix
of Adele's "Set Fire to the Rain" (written by
Adele Adkins and Fraser T. Smith; Smith also
original producer).

No stakes, no art. She has stakes now: a face
that cannot drift, a world that cannot slide,
and a record, 1,366 days from a concept post to
a first trailer, that anyone can check. The
montage is us showing our work.
